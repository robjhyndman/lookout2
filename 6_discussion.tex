\section{Discussion and conclusions}\label{sec:conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The bandwidth of the lookout algorithm was defined through the death diameters of a Vietoris-Rips filtration, which are the edge lengths of the Euclidean minimum spanning tree of the data. Working with the tree has allowed us to analyse the bandwidth and to modify the algorithm. The modified algorithm takes an upper quantile of the edge lengths as its bandwidth in place of the largest gap between consecutive edge lengths. The scaled quantile converges in probability to a constant for any density, so the bandwidth has the order of the spacing between neighbouring observations, whereas the longest edges of the tree grow without bound under heavy tails and shrink only as $\log\log n/\sqrt{\log n}$ for Gaussian data. The resulting kernel density estimator is not MISE-consistent, and at practical sample sizes in moderate dimensions its bandwidth exceeds the MISE-optimal one, because the quantile is set by the spacing in the sparse part of the sample, where anomalies are identified. In \cref{sec:bwablation} the ranking of the observations was insensitive to the bandwidth over a factor of eight, while the MISE-optimal bandwidth left too many observations isolated in six dimensions for the generalized Pareto distribution to be fitted. The two other modifications are a robust standardization with the minimum covariance determinant estimator, and a non-positive shape parameter in the generalized Pareto fit.

In the simulations the modified algorithm has a higher true positive rate than the original when the anomalies are close to the bulk of the data (Experiments 1 to 4), the same performance when they are well separated (Experiment 5, and Experiment S1 of the online Appendix), and a slightly lower true positive rate at the largest sample sizes of Experiment S2 of the online Appendix. It is ahead of HDoutliers, stray, KDEOS and RDOS in Experiments 3 and 4, and in Experiment 5 it matches RDOS on AUC while HDoutliers and stray never identify the anomaly. On the Old Faithful and wine reviews data it identifies the observations in the sparse regions, and fewer observations at the edge of the main cloud than the original.

The algorithm applies to any continuous multivariate data, and the experiments show good results up to 20 dimensions. It can be applied to problems that can be transformed to that setting, such as image anomaly detection through feature extraction or functional data through functional principal components. It assumes independent and identically distributed observations, so temporal or spatial data need pre-processing, for example by applying the algorithm to the residuals of a fitted model. In high dimensions kernel density estimation suffers from the curse of dimensionality, and dimension reduction such as principal components or dobin \citep{dobin} before applying lookout may help.

The minimum spanning tree is computed by the dual-tree Bor\r{u}vka algorithm of \citet{March2010} in $O(n\log n)$ time under mild conditions, and takes a few seconds for $n=10^6$ in two dimensions and about a minute for $n=10^5$ in ten (\cref{tab:timing}). The kernel density estimate dominates the cost. The exact leave-one-out estimate needs all pairwise distances and is practical only for $n$ up to about $10^4$, and the truncated sum over nearest neighbours takes about three minutes for $n=10^5$ in ten dimensions. A range search with the kernel support radius would give the exact estimate at a cost proportional to the number of observations inside the support, and is a natural improvement to the implementation. Two theoretical questions remain open. We have no formula for where the largest gap between consecutive edge lengths occurs, so the bandwidth of the original algorithm has not been analysed beyond the results on the longest edge. And the constant $q_\gamma(f)$ of \cref{prop:order} is defined through the component count of a Poisson process and is not explicit, although the bounds $t_-$ and $t_+$ are.
