\section{Introduction} \label{sec:intro}
% ------------------------------------------------------------

\cite{lookout} proposed the \textit{lookout} algorithm for anomaly detection using a kernel density estimate with bandwidth based on persistence homology. Anomalies are identified by fitting a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) to the negative logarithm of the leave-one-out kernel density estimates, and declaring points with low GPD probabilities as anomalies.

In this paper, we explore some of the theoretical properties of the lookout algorithm, particularly the choice of bandwidth based on the Rips death diameters, and the consistency of the kernel density estimates produced by this choice. This leads us to propose a modified lookout algorithm using a different bandwidth, although still based on the Rips death diameters. The resulting algorithm improves the performance and robustness of the original algorithm, under a broader range of conditions.

\subsection{The lookout algorithm for anomaly detection}

The \textit{lookout} algorithm of \cite{lookout} has four inputs: the dataset $\{\bm{y}_1,\dots,\bm{y}_n\}$, where each $\bm{y}_i \in \mathbb{R}^p$; a parameter $\beta \in (0,1)$ indicating the proportion of points to be used for fitting the Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD); a parameter $\alpha \in (0,1)$ signifying the cut-off threshold for determining an anomaly; and a logical variable \textit{unitize} indicating whether to scale the data. We briefly outline the steps below.

\begin{enumerate}
	\item If \textit{unitize}, then $\{\bm{y}_1,\dots,\bm{y}_n\}$ is scaled using min-max scaling so that the resulting data lies in the unit cube in $\mathbb{R}^p$.

  \item The persistence homology barcode of the data cloud is constructed for dimension zero using the Vietoris-Rips diameter \citep{ghrist2008barcodes}. From the barcode, the ordered death diameters $\{d_i\}_{i = 1}^n$ are obtained for connected components, and their successive differences are given by $\Delta d_i = d_{i+1} - d_i$ for $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$. The diameter corresponding to the lower point of the maximum $\Delta d_i$ is denoted $d_{*} = d_{i_*}$, where $i_{*} = \text{arg\! max} \{ \Delta d_i \}_{i=1}^n$.

	\item Using $\bm{H} = (d_*)^{2/p} \bm{I}$, kernel density estimates $\hat{f}(\bm{x}; \bm{H}) = \frac{1}{n} \sum_{j = 1}^n K_{\bm{H}}(\bm{x} - \bm{x}_j) $ are computed where $K$ is a multivariate kernel function \citep{chacon2018multivariate}. The kde values at the observations are given by $y_i = \hat{f}(\bm{x}_i; \bm{H})$, with the corresponding leave-one-out kde values given by $y_{-i} = \frac{1}{n-1} \big( n y_i - \bm{H}^{-1/2} K(\bm{0}) \big)$, $i=1,\dots,n$.

  \item Using the Peak Over Threshold (POT) approach \citep{coles2001introduction}, a Generalized Pareto Distribution (GPD) is fitted to largest $1-\beta$ of $\{ - \log y_i \}_{i = 1}^n$, providing estimates of the location, scale and shape parameters $(\mu, \sigma, \xi)$.

  \item The probability of each observation is calculated by applying the estimated GDP to the leave-one-out kde values: $P(- \log y_{-i} \mid \hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma}, \hat{\xi} )$. If $P(- \log y_{-i} \mid \hat{\mu}, \hat{\sigma}, \hat{\xi} ) < \beta\alpha$, $\bm{x}_i$ is declared anomalous.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Consistency of kde when bandwidths use Rips death diameters}

While \citet{lookout} demonstrated the results of their algorithm on a variety of simulated and real datasets, they did not provide a theoretical justification for their choice of bandwidth.

To obtain a consistent estimator of the underlying density, we need $\bm{H}$ to be positive definite and symmetric, and to satisfy the following conditions \citep[][p31--37]{chacon2018multivariate}:
\begin{itemize}
	\item All elements of $\bm{H}$ approach zero as $n\rightarrow\infty$; and
	\item $n^{-1}|\bm{H}|^{-1/2} \rightarrow 0$ as $n\rightarrow\infty$.
\end{itemize}
Thus, for the approach of \cite{lookout} to work, we need
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditions}
	d_*^{2/p}\rightarrow 0 \quad\text{and}\quad (Nd_*)^{-1}\rightarrow0\quad{as}\quad
	n\rightarrow\infty
\end{equation}
In the following section, we will explore the conditions under which these hold, and the implications for the lookout algorithm. In particular, we will show that the conditions are not satisfied for distributions with heavy tails.

A secondary consideration is that in using a diagonal matrix $\bm{H}$, we are ignoring the correlations between the variables. This is not a problem if the data are approximately uncorrelated, but can lead to suboptimal performance if the data have strong correlations. So in our modified lookout algorithm, we will use a rotation to ensure that the data have zero pairwise correlations.

\subsection{The modified lookout algorithm}

As a result of these theoretical considerations, we propose several modifications to the lookout algorithm.
\begin{enumerate}
	\item Rather than use the lower point of the largest gap in Rips death diameters, we propose to use an upper quantile of the Rips death diameters. This avoids the problem where anomalies affect the largest gap in the Rips diameters that cause a persistent homology death.
	\item We scale and rotate the data to ensure that the covariance matrix of the transformed data is approximately the identity matrix.
\end{enumerate}
Note that we do not need to estimate the density of the underlying distribution directly. Instead, we can estimate the density of a transformation of the data, and identify anomalies in the transformed space. Hence, there is no need to undo the scaling or rotation/

\subsection{Outline of the paper}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In \autoref{sec:theory}, we explore the theoretical properties of the lookout algorithm, particularly the choice of bandwidth based on Rips death diameters, and the consistency of the kernel density estimates produced by this choice. In  \autoref{sec:modified}, we propose a modified lookout algorithm that improves performance and robustness under a broader range of conditions. We provide some empirical experiments, demonstrating the performance of the modified lookout algorithm on simulated and real datasets, first in contrast to the original lookout (\autoref{sec:comparewitholderversion}), and then in contrast to other anomaly detection methods (\autoref{sec:compareWithOtherMethods}). Finally, in \autoref{sec:conclusion}, we summarize our results and discuss future work.
