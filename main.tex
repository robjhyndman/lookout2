\bigskip
\begin{abstract}
  The lookout algorithm identifies anomalies by fitting a generalized Pareto distribution to the largest surprisals of a leave-one-out kernel density estimate, with a bandwidth chosen from the data by persistent homology. The death diameters it uses are the edge lengths of the Euclidean minimum spanning tree of the data, and we use the tree to analyse the algorithm and to modify it. The bandwidth becomes an upper quantile of the edge lengths in place of the largest gap between consecutive edge lengths. Using limit theory for random geometric graphs, we show that the scaled quantile converges in probability to a constant for any density, so the bandwidth has the order of the spacing between neighbouring observations in the sparse part of the sample, whereas the longest edges grow without bound under heavy tails. The resulting density estimate is not consistent, and we argue that this is appropriate for anomaly detection. We also replace min-max scaling with a robust covariance standardization, and constrain the shape parameter of the generalized Pareto distribution. In simulations and two applications the modified algorithm improves on the original in most cases and compares well with other methods.
\end{abstract}
