## 1. Errors and inconsistencies

Most serious first. Each is fixed in the step named in brackets.

1. **Stale claims contradicting §2** [step 5]. Abstract (`main.tex:99`) says "Rips death diameters" and "shown to be consistent". `6_discussion.tex:5` says persistent homology, a consistent estimator, and the orthogonalized Gnanadesikan–Kettenring estimator, whereas §3.1 and the package use the minimum covariance determinant. `6_discussion.tex:13` attributes O(n log n) to Gower and Ross and describes a Vietoris–Rips computation; the strengths list there claims consistency and persistent homology. `1_introduction.tex:93` promises "matching lower and upper bounds" when `prop:order` now gives an exact limit; `1_introduction.tex:95` says "we adapt their methods ... shrink the radius to 0", which described the deleted Gaussian theorem.

2. **The undersmoothing narrative in §2.5–2.6 is asymptotic only** [step 2]. `2_mst.tex:111` says the bandwidth "undersmooths relative to MISE in every dimension"; `2_mst.tex:91` says the kernel window holds "a fixed number of neighbours" like a fixed-k estimator. Simulation with standard normal data, γ = 0.98 and the package's type-8 quantile, comparing h_n with the normal-reference bandwidth h_NR = (4/(m+2))^{1/(m+4)} n^{-1/(m+4)} in the same per-coordinate-sd units:

   | m | n | n^{1/m} h_n | h_n / h_NR | points in kernel window at the mode |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 2 | 1000 | 12.4 | 1.24 | 341 |
   | 2 | 4000 | 11.3 | 0.71 | 349 |
   | 2 | 16000 | 12.1 | 0.48 | 411 |
   | 3 | 1000 | 7.7 | 2.13 | 682 |
   | 3 | 16000 | 8.4 | 1.37 | 2065 |
   | 5 | 1000 | 5.8 | 3.34 | 997 |
   | 5 | 16000 | 6.4 | 2.87 | 7999 |

   The scaled quantile stabilises, so `prop:order` is fine. The ratio drops below one for m = 2 beyond about n = 2000; for m = 5 it decays as n^{-4/45} and never reaches one in practice. The window count is n|H|^{1/2} f(x), proportional to f(x), so it is hundreds to thousands in the bulk and small in the tails, not "a fixed number of neighbours". Honest reading: a 98% quantile is set by the sparsest 2% of the sample, so the bandwidth is tuned to tail spacing and is coarse for the bulk in moderate dimensions. Still defensible for anomaly detection, but the text must say this and the bandwidth ablation (step 3a) has to back it.

3. **α is wrong** [step 4]. `3_newlookout.tex:71` says α = 0.001; `_targets.R` and the package default are 0.01. Fix the text (or rerun everything at 0.001, which is not recommended).

4. **Algorithm 1 is a hybrid, and so is "old lookout" in the experiments** [step 4]. From the CRAN archive: lookout 0.1.0 (Feb 2021) and 0.1.4 (Oct 2022) multiply the largest-gap death radius by √5 in every dimension, threshold the GPD at the min(0.9, 1−α) quantile, report p_i without the (1−β) factor, and default to α = 0.05. The median restriction on the gap search is in 0.1.4 but not 0.1.0. The experiments run the current package with `old_version = TRUE`, which keeps min–max scaling and the gap rule but uses √(m+4), the (1−β) factor, α = 0.01 and no shape constraint. That comparison isolates the three modifications and is the right one, but §4 must say so, and Algorithm 1 must match the 2022 paper (with a footnote on the median restriction) rather than neither version.

5. **Experiment text versus code** [step 4].
   - Experiment 2 uses 20 replicates (`R/Experiment2.R`, `reps <- 20`), text says 10 (`4_experiments.tex:40`).
   - Experiment 3 flips the sign of μ_{i2} for half the anomalies to make a full ring (`R/Experiment3.R`); `4_experiments.tex:55` gives only the positive root.

## 2. Theory: what was verified and what to simplify

Verified against the source papers on 18 September 2026 (details also in the memory file `lookout2-verified-sources`).

- **Mitsche & Penrose (2021)**, arXiv 2006.14915v1 (the only arXiv version): Theorem 3.13 is the LLN for the number of components of G(X_n, r_n) in the thermodynamic limit, obtained from their Theorem 2.1; it holds for any diffuse distribution, convergence is complete and in L², and the limit function ρ(λ) = E|C_o(λ)|^{-1} is our κ. Proposition 2.3(ii) gives continuity of ρ and (v) monotonicity. **Still to do:** confirm the theorem numbers in the published AAP version (31(6), 2721–2771); the TODO comment at `7_proofs.tex:17` stays until then.
- **Penrose & Yukich (2003)**, preprint arXiv math/0107149: Theorem 2.1 imposes no condition on the density, only stabilization and a moment bound on the functional, so the nearest-neighbour limit in the last paragraph of the proof of `prop:order` is justified. Theorem 1.1 is as cited in §2.4 (a.s., ∫ f^{1−1/m} < ∞, moment of order r > m/(m−1)). Theorem 2.3(i) with φ = 1_{[0,t]} gives the L² LLN for the empirical distribution of rescaled MST edge lengths for any density, attributed to Penrose (1996, Ann. Probab. 24, 1903–1925): a one-sentence cross-check worth adding.
- **Penrose (1997)** is the unit square only, as the text says; it also shows the longest MST edge equals the largest nearest-neighbour link with probability tending to one. **Penrose (1998)**: for standard normal points in R^m, (2 log n)^{1/2} M_n − b_n → Gumbel with b_n ~ (m−1) log log n, also for the nearest-neighbour graph, so the longest edge is of order (m−1) log log n / √(2 log n). **Hsing & Rootzén (2005)** is "Extremes on trees", Ann. Probab. 33(1), 413–444, R² with unbounded support (elliptical, Weibull-like margins); the title given in `MST_anomaly_detection_references.pdf` is wrong.

Simplifications to make in step 1:

- **Proof of `prop:order`, Step 2**: replace the half-page continuity argument for κ by citing Mitsche & Penrose Proposition 2.3(ii) and (v); keep the three-line strict-decrease argument, which uniqueness needs. Keep the bounds t_± as they are.
- **Replace the proof of `cex:fattails`** and strengthen the statement. New statement: for every m ≥ 1, with R_n = n^{3/4}, s_n = n^{1/8}, ω_n = n − ⌊n^{1/4}/4⌋, P(e_{(ω_n)} > s_n) → 1; in particular the top n^{1/4}/4 edge lengths diverge in probability. Proof: (i) N_n = #{i : ‖y_i‖ > R_n} is binomial with success probability of order n^{-3/4} (tail probability p_{[R,∞)} ∈ (c_m s_{m−1}/(2R), c_m s_{m−1}/R) for R ≥ 1), so by Chebyshev N_n ≥ n^{1/4}/2 with probability → 1. (ii) Let Y_n be the number of pairs within distance s_n among points with ‖y_i‖ > R_n − s_n. E[Y_n] ≤ (n²/2) · P(‖x‖ > R_n − s_n) · sup_{‖x‖ > R_n − s_n} P(y ∈ B(x, s_n)) ≤ C n² s_n^m R_n^{-(m+2)} = C n^{1/2 − 5m/8} → 0, so P(Y_n = 0) → 1. (iii) On {N_n ≥ n^{1/4}/2, Y_n = 0} every point beyond R_n is isolated at scale s_n, so C_n(s_n) > n − ω_n and e_{(ω_n)} > s_n. Drop `lem:bound edges` and `lem:bound points`; keep the Adler et al. citation as the source of the idea, not as machinery.
- **§2.3, last paragraph**: add Hsing & Rootzén beside Penrose 1998. **§2.4**: use the Penrose 1998 rate to say that even under Gaussian tails the longest edges sit far above n^{-1/m}, so the largest-gap rule has the right order only if the gap falls well below the top of the edge-length distribution.
- **Cut** the total-length paragraph in §2.4 (Steele 1988, Penrose–Yukich Theorem 1.1, Yukich 1998); the counterexample carries the argument alone.
